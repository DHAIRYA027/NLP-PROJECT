Our college was established with the goal of providing quality education to students from all backgrounds. The campus spans several acres and includes academic blocks, a central library, hostels, sports facilities, and a cafeteria. Students can take part in a wide range of clubs and societies covering technology, arts, music, and social service.

The academic year is divided into two semesters. Each semester includes regular classes, internal assessments, and a final examination. Attendance is mandatory for all students and is tracked through the student portal. Professors hold office hours every week for students who need extra help with coursework.

The library houses a large collection of books, journals, and digital resources. Students can borrow books for two weeks at a time and renew them online if no one else has requested the same title. Quiet study rooms are available on the upper floor for exam preparation.

The placement cell works closely with companies to organize internships and full time job opportunities for final year students. Training sessions on resume building, aptitude tests, and interview skills are conducted every year before the placement season begins. Many students also pursue higher studies after graduation.

Hostel life is an important part of the college experience. Students share rooms with roommates and take part in cultural events organized by the hostel committee. Mess committees manage the food menu and collect feedback from students every month to improve the quality of meals.

The college encourages students to participate in technical festivals, hackathons, and workshops organized throughout the year. These events help students build practical skills beyond the classroom and network with peers from other colleges. Faculty members often mentor student teams for competitions at the state and national level.

The administration office handles matters related to fees, certificates, scholarships, and student records. Scholarships are available for students based on merit and financial need. Students facing any issue can approach the student welfare office for guidance and support.

Sports facilities on campus include a gymnasium, basketball court, football ground, and indoor games room. The sports committee organizes inter college tournaments every year and encourages students to maintain a healthy lifestyle alongside their studies. Annual sports day brings together students from every department.

Admission to the college begins every year in the month of June and continues until all seats are filled. Candidates are required to submit their twelfth grade marksheet, a valid identity proof, and their entrance exam scorecard through the online admissions portal. The admissions office reviews applications on a rolling basis and shortlisted candidates are invited for a counselling session. Seats under the management quota are limited and are allotted separately from the merit based seats. Students transferring from another institution can apply for lateral entry admission directly into the second year, subject to eligibility criteria set by the university. The admission helpline remains open on working days for candidates who need assistance with the application process.

The library is one of the busiest places on campus, especially during the weeks leading up to examinations. It remains open from eight in the morning until nine at night from Monday to Saturday, and stays closed on Sundays and public holidays. During examination season the library extends its hours and stays open until midnight to accommodate students who need a quiet place to study. The ground floor houses the general reading collection while the upper floor is reserved for silent individual study. Students can borrow up to five books at a time using their student identity card and can renew them online through the library portal if no other student has placed a request on the same title. The reference section and digital archive are available for research projects and cannot be taken outside the building.

Examinations are conducted twice every academic year, once at the end of each semester. The mid semester examinations are usually held around the eighth week of classes and carry a smaller weight toward the final grade compared to the end semester examination. The definitive exam timetable, commonly called the date sheet, is published on the official college website roughly one month before the examinations begin, giving students enough time to plan their revision schedule. Admit cards must be downloaded from the student portal before the first exam and carried to every examination hall along with a valid college identity card. Students who miss an exam due to a genuine and documented reason may apply for a supplementary examination, which is typically scheduled a few weeks after the regular exam session concludes. Results are usually declared within four to six weeks after the last paper of the semester.

Fee payment is one of the most common queries the helpdesk receives every semester. The tuition fee, along with any applicable hostel and mess charges, must be paid within the first two weeks after the semester begins. Payments can be made online through the student portal using a debit card, credit card, or net banking, and a receipt is generated automatically for every successful transaction. Students who are unable to pay the full amount at once can apply for an installment plan through the accounts office, provided the request is submitted before the original due date. A late fee penalty is added automatically for any payment made after the deadline, and continued delay can result in a temporary hold on examination registration. Refunds for withdrawn admissions are processed according to the policy published by the university, with the refund percentage decreasing the later the withdrawal request is submitted.

Hostel life is an important part of the overall college experience for many students. Applications for hostel accommodation open shortly after admission is confirmed and rooms are generally allotted on a first come first served basis, although preference is sometimes given to students coming from farther away. Most hostel rooms are shared between two or three students and each hostel block has its own mess with a rotating weekly menu managed by a student mess committee. The curfew for entering the hostel is set at ten in the evening on weekdays and extended slightly on weekends, and gate passes are required for students who need to stay out later for a legitimate reason. Wifi is available throughout the hostel premises, though the connection can slow down considerably during peak hours in the evening when most students are online at the same time. Students who wish to change their room or their roommate can submit a request to the hostel warden, who reviews such requests once every semester.

The placement cell begins its main recruitment drive in the seventh semester and continues actively through the eighth semester for students who have not yet secured an offer. Companies visiting the campus range from large product based technology firms to service based consultancies and a smaller number of core engineering companies, depending on the branch of study. Before the placement season starts, the training and placement office conducts several sessions on resume building, aptitude test practice, group discussions, and mock interviews to help students prepare. Students interested in an internship rather than a full time role can also register separately, since many summer internship offers eventually convert into a full time pre placement offer. The placement cell publishes a statistics report every year summarizing the number of students placed, the highest package offered, and the average package across branches, which is a useful reference for prospective students and their families.

Scholarships at the college are broadly divided into merit based and need based categories. Merit scholarships are awarded automatically to students who secure a rank above a certain cutoff in their entrance examination or maintain a high grade point average in previous semesters, and do not usually require a separate application. Need based financial aid, on the other hand, requires students to submit an application along with supporting income documents to the financial aid office, and awards are reviewed on a case by case basis. Scholarships reserved for students from reserved categories are processed through the National Scholarship Portal maintained by the government, and students are strongly encouraged to complete their registration on that portal well before the annual deadline. Scholarship recipients are generally required to maintain a minimum attendance percentage and academic performance in order to have their scholarship renewed for the following year.

Getting around campus and the surrounding city is made easier through the college transport service. College buses run on a set number of routes covering the main residential areas of the city, with one trip in the morning before classes begin and another in the evening after classes end. Students who wish to use the bus service need to apply for a bus pass at the transport office at the start of each semester, and passes must be renewed every semester since they are not valid indefinitely. On weekends the bus frequency is reduced significantly and only a couple of routes continue to operate. A limited number of vehicles are also available for private parking near the academic block, though space fills up quickly during peak hours.

Wifi and general information technology support are handled by a dedicated helpdesk located in the main academic block. Students experiencing connectivity issues, whether in the hostel or in classrooms, are asked to raise a ticket describing the problem so the technical team can look into it. New devices need to be registered on the campus network before they can connect, which can be done from the student portal using a valid roll number and password. Password reset requests for the student portal or college email account are usually processed within a few hours during working days. Lab printers occasionally run out of toner or paper, and students are asked to report this to the lab assistant rather than attempting to fix the hardware themselves.

The college takes student complaints and grievances seriously and maintains a formal grievance redressal process for this purpose. Students can submit a complaint anonymously or with their identity attached through the online grievance portal, and each complaint is assigned a tracking number so students can follow up on its status. Complaints involving ragging or harassment are handled with the highest priority by a dedicated anti ragging committee and are kept strictly confidential throughout the investigation. Academic grievances, such as disputes over grading or attendance, are typically first raised with the concerned faculty member before being escalated to the head of department if the issue remains unresolved. Feedback about teaching quality can also be submitted at the end of every semester through an anonymous course feedback form.

The administration office serves as the central point of contact for most non academic matters and can be reached by phone or email on all working days between nine in the morning and five in the evening. Each department also has its own office where the head of department and faculty coordinators can be reached for subject specific queries. For emergencies outside of regular working hours, a separate emergency contact number is displayed at the main gate and in every hostel block. International students are assigned a dedicated coordinator who assists with visa documentation, orientation, and other matters specific to studying away from home.

Beyond academics, the college encourages students to take part in technical festivals, cultural events, hackathons, and workshops that run throughout the year. These events are usually organized by student clubs and societies with support from the faculty and give students a chance to build practical skills outside the regular classroom setting. Sports facilities on campus include a gymnasium, a basketball court, a football ground, and an indoor games room, and the sports committee organizes inter college tournaments as well as an annual sports day that brings together students from every department and every year of study.
